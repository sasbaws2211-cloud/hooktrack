What makes a good hook in short-form video? How to measure it

20 October 2026 · 6 min read

On TikTok, Reels and Shorts, people decide in about three seconds whether to keep watching. The words and the first shot in those seconds are your hook. Here is what it is, how it is measured, and how to judge yours even when the platform does not hand you the numbers.

What is a hook?

A hook is the opening of a video: the first line you say or show on screen, plus the first shot. Its only job is to stop the scroll and make someone want to see the next few seconds. The rest of the video, the body and the payoff, has a different job: keep them and make them act.

What "hook rate" means

In advertising, hook rate (also called thumb-stop rate) is usually the share of people who see a video and watch at least three seconds. A related number, hold rate, shows how many of those go on to watch further. Read together, they point to the problem: a strong hook with weak hold means the opening oversells and the body disappoints. A weak hook with strong hold means the idea is fine but the opening is easy to skip. Segwise explains this split well.

Vendor guides often quote rough targets. For example, NestScale puts a good TikTok hook rate around 30 to 40 percent and a weak one under about 25 percent. These are vendor rules of thumb, not official platform numbers, and the sources do not agree with each other. Use them as a loose guide only.

The catch: you often cannot see it

Hook rate needs impressions and three-second plays. Some platform dashboards show them to the account owner, but they are not available for videos on accounts you only track from outside, and they are shown differently on each platform. So most people need another way to judge a hook.

What you can measure instead

Even without retention data, three numbers tell you a lot about how well an opening worked, if you compare them with the same account's usual results:

  1. Views against the account's normal. If a video travels far beyond what the account usually gets, the opening stopped the scroll. This is the closest stand-in for hook strength. Here is how to calculate it.
  2. Saves and shares per view. People save or share what they want to keep or pass on. If views are high but saves and shares are low, the payoff did not land.
  3. Comments per view. Comments, especially ones asking where to buy or how to get it, show the video made people act.

A simple way to read the pattern

  • High views, low saves and shares: the opening works. Rework the body or the payoff.
  • Low views, high saves and comments: the idea works, but the opening is weak. Rework the first three seconds.
  • Low on everything: change the angle, not only the wording.
  • High on everything: repeat this angle with fresh versions.

Habits that improve hooks

  • Open on the point. Skip greetings and warm-ups. Put the most interesting claim first.
  • Be specific. "I lost 9 kg in 60 days with one change" beats "weight loss tips".
  • Make one change at a time. If you rewrite the opening, the body and the music together, you will not know which worked.
  • Re-make your winners. A hook that beat the account's normal once is more likely to work again than a brand-new idea.
  • Keep a log. Write down each hook and its result, so patterns show up.

Questions

How long should a hook be?

About the first three seconds. Whatever you say or show in that time is the hook.

Can I measure hook rate on someone else's video?

Not directly, because impressions and three-second plays are private to the account owner. Compare views, saves, shares and comments with the account's own normal instead.

Is a good hook enough to make a video go viral?

No. A strong hook gets the first seconds of attention. The body and payoff decide whether people stay, save and share.
