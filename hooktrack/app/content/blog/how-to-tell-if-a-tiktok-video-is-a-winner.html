How to tell if a TikTok video is a winner (not just lucky)

20 October 2026 · 6 min read

Your video got 80,000 views. Is that good? It depends on whose account it is. For an account that usually gets 5,000, it is a breakout. For one that usually gets 150,000, it is a miss. The number alone tells you almost nothing.

The idea in one line: judge every video against its own account's normal, not against other people's numbers or a general benchmark.

Why raw views mislead

Views depend on the size of the audience, how often you post, the niche, and plain luck. Comparing a video to a "good" number you read somewhere ignores all of that. Two accounts can post equally strong videos and see ten-fold different views.

Analytics tools that rank videos use the same fix. For example, vidIQ's outlier score compares a video with the channel's typical performance: about 1x means normal, and a much higher number means it stood out.

Step 1: find the account's normal

Take the last 30 videos on the account that have been up for at least two days (newer ones are still collecting views). Put the views in order and take the middle one. That is the median, and it is better than an average because one viral hit cannot drag it up.

You need at least five older videos for this to mean anything. With fewer, any call is a guess.

Step 2: divide

Take the new video's views and divide by the normal. That gives a multiple:

  • 2x or more: a winner. It did at least twice what the account usually does.
  • About 0.7x to 1.2x: normal. Nothing to change.
  • Above normal but under 2x: it worked a bit. Worth strengthening.
  • Below 0.7x: weaker than usual. Rework the opening.
  • 0.5x or less: a dud.

Example: an account's normal is 12,000. A video with 30,000 views is 2.5x, a winner. A video with 5,000 is 0.4x, a dud.

Step 3: do not judge too early

A video from yesterday is still gathering views, so its number will keep moving. Wait at least two days before you call it, and a week for a firm verdict. The exception is a very strong first day, which is worth acting on while it is hot.

Step 4: one bad video is not a pattern

Every account has misses. Do not stop an idea because of one dud. Stop it when it is weak again and again, for example two of the last three videos on that angle. A single miss is a reason to tweak the first three seconds, not to start over.

Step 5: look past views

A video can get fewer views but a lot of saves, shares and comments from people who really wanted it. Compare those against the account's normal too. Together they tell you whether people only watched or actually wanted to keep it or pass it on. A video with strong reach but few saves and shares usually has a good opening and a weak payoff. A video with low reach but lots of action has a good idea and a weak opening.

Doing this by hand

You can do all of this in a spreadsheet: list the last 30 videos, take the median, divide, and colour the results. It works, but it breaks down once you have many accounts and have to repeat it every week.

Questions

What is a good number of views on TikTok?

There is no universal number. A good video is one that beats what that account normally gets. Use the median of the account's last 30 videos as the yardstick.

How long should I wait before judging a video?

Wait at least two days before calling it, and about a week for a settled verdict. Early views are still growing.

Does this work for Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts?

Yes. The same idea works on any short-form platform, as long as you compare each video with that account's own history.
