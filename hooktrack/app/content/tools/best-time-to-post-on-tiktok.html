Why a generic "best time to post on TikTok" chart can mislead you

A chart that says "post at 7 pm on Tuesday" averages accounts with very different audiences, countries and niches. A cooking account, a gaming account and a study-tips account have viewers who are online at different times. The only dependable answer is in your own results, so the analyzer compares each of your TikToks with your account's normal.

How this analyzer finds your best time to post on TikTok

  1. It finds your normal: the median views across the videos you paste.
  2. It shows every video as a multiple of that normal, so a 200,000-view video and a 4,000-view video are judged on your own scale.
  3. It groups your videos by day of the week and by three-hour window, and takes the median of each group.
  4. It ranks the slots that have at least two videos, and tells you how much to trust the answer.

It uses the median so one viral video cannot make a single time slot look better than it really is, which happens often on TikTok.

How to get your dates and views from TikTok

Open your TikTok analytics (in the app's creator tools, or TikTok Studio on a computer) and look at your list of videos. Copy the date and time each one was posted and its view count. Paste one video per line in a format like 2026-10-04 18:30, 4200. Dates such as Oct 4, 2026 6:30 PM work too, as long as the year is included. Menu names change over time, so search TikTok's help if you cannot find the posting time. TikTok's own analytics may also show when your followers are most active, if your account has enough data, which is worth comparing with your results here.

How many TikToks do you need?

At least 8 to run the analyzer, and 20 or more for a pattern you can trust. With 30 to 60 you can see clearly whether timing matters for your account. With only a few, a slot can look great because of one lucky video, and the analyzer will tell you so.

Test your best time before you change your schedule

  1. Pick your top two or three slots from the results.
  2. For three or four weeks, post similar videos at those times.
  3. Compare them with your normal. If they keep beating it, keep them. If not, the video matters more than the time.

If your best and worst slots are close, that is useful too: it means you can post when it suits you and spend your effort on the first seconds of each video.

Time zones

The analyzer uses the times exactly as you type them. Use the time zone you plan in, and convert first if most of your viewers are in another country.

Questions

What is the best time to post on TikTok?

It depends on your audience, and an average chart will not tell you. Paste your own TikToks into the analyzer above to see which days and times did best for your account.

Is this analyzer free?

Yes. It is free, needs no account and runs in your browser. Nothing you paste is uploaded or stored.

Does it connect to my TikTok account?

No. You copy the dates and views yourself, so it never connects to TikTok.

Does the time of day really change TikTok views?

Sometimes, but usually less than the video itself. The analyzer shows whether timing makes a visible difference for your account, and how sure that answer is.

What if every time slot looks the same?

Then timing is not holding your videos back. Put your effort into the hook, the first few seconds, instead.
