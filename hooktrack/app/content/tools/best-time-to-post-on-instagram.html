This free analyzer uses your Reels or posts instead: paste the dates, times and views, and it shows which days and hours did best for your account. Why a generic "best time to post on Instagram" chart can mislead you

A chart that says "post at 11 am on Wednesday" averages accounts with very different audiences, countries and niches. A fitness coach, a restaurant and a fashion shop have followers who are online at different times. The dependable answer is in your own results, so the analyzer compares each of your videos with your account's normal.

How this analyzer finds your best time to post on Instagram

  1. It finds your normal: the median views across the videos you paste.
  2. It shows every video as a multiple of that normal, so a 50,000-view Reel and a 3,000-view Reel are judged on your own scale.
  3. It groups them by day of the week and by three-hour window, and takes the median of each group.
  4. It ranks the slots that have at least two videos, and tells you how much to trust the answer.

It uses the median so one Reel that gets picked up by the Explore feed cannot make a single time slot look better than it really is.

How to get your dates and views from Instagram

Instagram's own analytics, called Insights, are available on professional accounts (creator or business). Open your professional dashboard, look at your Reels or posts, and copy the date and time each was shared with its view count. Paste one per line in a format like 2026-10-04 18:30, 4200. Dates such as Oct 4, 2026 6:30 PM work too, as long as the year is included. Menu names change over time, so search Instagram's help if you cannot find them. Insights may also show when your followers are most active, if your account has enough data, which is worth comparing with your results here.

Check Reels and feed posts separately

Reels and feed posts reach people differently and usually have very different normals. If you post both, run one check for your Reels and another for your feed posts. Mixing them makes the pattern meaningless for either.

How many do you need?

At least 8 to run the analyzer, and 20 or more for a pattern you can trust. With only a few, a slot can look great because of one lucky Reel, and the analyzer will tell you so.

Test your best time before you change your schedule

  1. Pick your top two or three slots from the results.
  2. For three or four weeks, share similar Reels or posts at those times.
  3. Compare them with your normal. If they keep beating it, keep them. If not, the content matters more than the time.

Time zones

The analyzer uses the times exactly as you type them. Use the time zone you plan in, and convert first if most of your followers are in another country.

Questions

What is the best time to post on Instagram?

It depends on your followers, and an average chart will not tell you. Paste your own Reels or posts into the analyzer above to see which days and times did best for your account.

Is this analyzer free?

Yes. It is free, needs no account and runs in your browser. Nothing you paste is uploaded or stored.

Does it connect to my Instagram account?

No. You copy the dates and views yourself, so it never connects to Instagram.

Does the time of day really change Instagram views?

Sometimes, but usually less than the content itself. The analyzer shows whether timing makes a visible difference for your account, and how sure that answer is.

Can I use it for Stories or feed posts?

It works for any list of dates and views, but compare like with like. Check Reels, feed posts and Stories separately.
