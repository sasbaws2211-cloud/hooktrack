Why there is no single best Reel length

A quick transition video, a tutorial and a story need different lengths. Advice such as "keep Reels under 15 seconds" or "use the full length" averages accounts that have nothing in common. The dependable answer is in your own results, so the analyzer compares each Reel with your account's normal.

How the Reel length analyzer works

  1. It finds your normal: the median views across the Reels you paste.
  2. It shows each Reel as a multiple of that normal.
  3. It groups them into length ranges, such as under 15 seconds, 15 to 30 seconds and 30 to 45 seconds, and takes the median of each.
  4. It ranks the ranges with at least two Reels, compares the length of your best and weakest Reels, and checks whether longer or shorter Reels tend to do better for you.

It uses the median so one Reel that gets picked up by the Explore feed cannot make a whole length range look better than it really is.

How to get your lengths and views from Instagram

Instagram's own analytics, called Insights, are available on professional accounts (creator or business). Open your professional dashboard and look at your Reels. For each one, note its length and its view count. Paste one per line in a format like 0:34, 4200, or add a title first: Packing tips, 0:28, 8200. Lengths like 45 (seconds), 1:30 and 1m 30s work. Menu names change over time, so search Instagram's help if you cannot find them.

Check Reels on their own

Keep Reels and feed videos in separate checks, because they reach people differently and usually have very different normals.

How many Reels do you need?

At least 10 to run the analyzer, and 20 or more for a pattern you can trust. Use Reels that have had a few days to collect views. With only a few, a length range can look great because of one lucky Reel, and the analyzer will say so.

What to do with your answer

  1. Make your next few Reels the length of your best range.
  2. Keep the topic and style similar, so you are testing length and not content.
  3. Run the analyzer again after three or four weeks. If the pattern holds, keep it.

If every range looks the same, length is not your problem. Spend your effort on the first seconds.
