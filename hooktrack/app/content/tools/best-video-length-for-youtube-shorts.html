Why there is no single best length for Shorts

YouTube allows Shorts of up to 3 minutes, so there is real room to choose. A quick joke, a tutorial and a short story need different lengths, and tips like "stay under 30 seconds" average channels that have little in common. The dependable answer is in your own results, so the analyzer compares each Short with your channel's normal.

How the Shorts length analyzer works

  1. It finds your normal: the median views across the Shorts you paste.
  2. It shows each Short as a multiple of that normal.
  3. It groups them into length ranges, such as under 15 seconds, 15 to 30 seconds, 30 to 45 seconds and up to 3 minutes, and takes the median of each.
  4. It ranks the ranges with at least two Shorts, compares the length of your best and weakest Shorts, and checks whether longer or shorter Shorts tend to do better for you.

It uses the median so one Short that takes off cannot make a whole length range look better than it really is.

How to get your lengths and views from YouTube Studio

Open YouTube Studio and go to your content list, then filter to Shorts. For each one, note its length and its view count. Paste one per line in a format like 0:34, 4200, or add a title first: Quick edit tip, 0:28, 6400. Lengths like 45 (seconds), 1:30 and 1m 30s work. Layouts change over time, so search YouTube's help if the list looks different.

Keep Shorts and long videos separate

Shorts and regular videos reach people differently and usually have very different normals. Run Shorts on their own here. For regular videos, use the YouTube analytics checker.

How many Shorts do you need?

At least 10 to run the analyzer, and 20 or more for a pattern you can trust. Shorts are quick to make, so aim for 30 or more if you can. Use Shorts that have had a few days to collect views. With only a few, a length range can look great because of one lucky Short, and the analyzer will say so.

What to do with your answer

  1. Make your next few Shorts the length of your best range.
  2. Keep the topic and style similar, so you are testing length and not content.
  3. Run the analyzer again after three or four weeks. If the pattern holds, keep it.

If every range looks the same, length is not what is limiting your Shorts. Put your effort into the first seconds.
