What the score checks

The score is seven simple checks, each worth a set number of points. They are common advice about what holds attention in the first seconds, so a hook that misses one is not broken, it just has something you could improve.

CheckPointsWhat it looks for
Length20About 6 to 14 words, which can be said aloud in around 3 seconds.
Opening words15No greeting or warm-up such as "hey guys" or "in this video".
Clear pattern25It asks a question, uses a number, warns, shows a result, teases something hidden, sets up a contrast or makes a bold claim. Two or more patterns score highest.
Specifics15A number, a timeframe or a named thing, which feels more believable than a general claim.
Talks to the viewer10"You" or "your", or a direct command.
Open question10Something is left unanswered, which gives a reason to keep watching.
Plain words5Short, simple words that are easy to take in quickly.

The score is a checklist, not a prediction. A high score does not mean a video will get views, and a low score does not mean it will fail. Many successful videos break these habits, and the checks cannot see your audience, your topic or your face on screen.

How to use the scorer well

  1. Write three or four versions of the same hook and paste them all. Comparing is more useful than a single number.
  2. Fix the biggest missing check first. The list under each hook shows where the points went, and the "Try this" box gives a pattern to start from.
  3. Say it aloud. A hook that reads well can still be hard to say in 3 seconds.
  4. Then post it and measure. The score is only a starting point. What matters is how the video does against your own normal.

How to tell whether a hook really worked

A view count on its own does not say much, because it means different things on different accounts. Comparing a video with the median of your own recent videos shows which hooks beat your normal, so you can repeat them. Questions

What makes a good hook for a short video?

A good hook makes the viewer want the next few seconds. It usually gets to the point at once, is specific, speaks to the viewer and leaves something open that the video then answers. There is no formula that always works, so treat any rule as a starting point.

How long should a hook be?

Around 6 to 14 words is a common rule of thumb, because it can be said aloud in about 3 seconds. The scorer gives full marks for length in that range and fewer points for very short or very long lines.

Does a higher score mean more views?

No. The score only checks the wording of the first line against common advice. It cannot see your audience, your topic, your video or the platform's recommendations, so it cannot predict views. Use it to improve a hook, then post it and compare the result with your own normal.

Does this use AI, and is my hook stored?

No to both. The scorer is a set of fixed rules that run in your browser. Nothing you type is sent to us or anywhere else.

Does it work for TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts?

Yes. The checks are about the wording of an opening line, which applies to all three. Platform rules differ, so check each platform's own guidance for anything specific to it.

Can I score a title or a caption?

You can paste any short line, but the scorer is built for the first thing a viewer hears or reads on screen. Titles and captions have different jobs, so the result is only a rough guide for them.
