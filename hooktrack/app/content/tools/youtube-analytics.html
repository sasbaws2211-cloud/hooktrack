How this YouTube analytics checker works

A view count only means something next to your channel's usual numbers. 20,000 views is a breakout on a channel that normally gets 4,000 and an average day on one that gets 25,000. The checker takes the median views of the videos you paste as your normal and shows each one as a multiple of it.

  • Scale: 2× your normal or more.
  • Hold: in between, a normal result.
  • Tweak: half your normal or less.

The median is the fairer yardstick. Shorts in particular can have one outlier that sits far above the rest, and an average would hide how your other videos did.

Where to find analytics in YouTube

Your channel's analytics are in YouTube Studio. Open the Analytics section, or the content list, and copy the views for each video you want to check. Paste them above one per line, with a short title in front if you like, for example Quick edit tutorial, 6400. Layouts change over time, so use YouTube's help and search for "YouTube Studio analytics" if it looks different.

Check Shorts and long videos separately

Shorts and regular videos reach people differently and usually have very different normals. If you post both, run two checks: one with only your Shorts and one with only your long videos. Mixing them makes your normal meaningless for either.

What to look at in YouTube analytics besides views

  • How long people watched. For Shorts, the first seconds decide whether people stay.
  • Likes, comments and shares. They show the video landed, not only that it was shown.
  • Subscribers gained. A video that adds subscribers is worth repeating even if its views are only average.

Give each video a few days before you judge it.

What to do with the result

For each Scale video, write down the opening line and the structure, then make a close copy with new specifics. For one Tweak, change the first seconds or the title before anything else. Three duds in a row, and the checker will flag it, usually means the format needs a rethink.
